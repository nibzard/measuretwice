# Handoff completeness

Status: proposed experiment. No evaluator results or qualification evidence exist for this brief.

## Purpose

Test whether measuretwice predicts when a person cannot continue a task from its handoff.
Compare check outcomes with observed questions and blocked work.

## Case and checks

Each case contains the task, the handoff, supplied reference material, and the recipient's stated role and access.

- Does the handoff identify the current state and next action?
- Does it state decisions, unresolved questions, and relevant constraints?
- Does it identify necessary artifacts and how the recipient can access them?
- Does it explain how the recipient can verify completion?

Pass means the handoff contains the information required for the stated next action.
Fail means a required fact is absent or contradicted.
Review means task scope, recipient access, or the next action is unclear.
An execution failure remains an error.

## First experiment

1. Select bounded tasks that another person can safely continue.
2. Define observable blockers and acceptable clarification effort before the trial.
3. Assess each handoff before the recipient attempts the task.
4. Record clarification questions, missing facts, and whether the next action succeeds.
5. Have a reviewer distinguish handoff omissions from unrelated execution problems.
6. Compare direct evaluation with measuretwice on matching cases and observed outcomes.

Keep evaluator answers hidden from recipients until their attempts finish.
Keep later questions and outcomes out of evaluator inputs.
Group related tasks and recipients when separating development and validation evidence.

## Decision and limits

Continue if failed checks predict actionable omissions at an acceptable review cost.
The owner must state acceptable error and review limits before fitting a policy.
Account for recipient knowledge and access when interpreting outcomes.
Successful completion by one recipient does not establish universal handoff quality.
